Short Bio: Heung-No Lee received the B.S., M.S., and Ph.D. degrees in electrical engineering from the University of California, Los Angeles, in 1993, 1994, and 1999, respectively. His Ph.D. dissertation, advised by Professor Gregory J. Pottie, was titled Adaptive Diversity Combining, Equalization and Sequence Decoding for Time-Varying Dispersive Channels. He was a Research Staff Member at HRL Laboratories from 1999 to 2002 and an Assistant Professor in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Pittsburgh from 2002 to 2008. Since 2009, he has been a Professor at the School of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science, GIST.

At GIST, Professor Lee has served in major research and leadership roles, including Director of the ITRC Blockchain Intelligence Convergence Research Center, Director of the Blockchain Internet Economic Research Center, Director of the Intelligent Sensors Research Center, and former Director of the AI Research Institute. He is also CEO of LiberVance Co., Ltd., a GIST professor start-up venture, and a member of the 3rd Artificial Intelligence Industry Committee. His work spans machine learning, computational intelligence, communications, information theory, signal processing, intelligent sensors, networks, and blockchain technologies.

Professor Lee has received numerous honors, including the Prime Minister's Commendation at the 55th Science Day in 2022, the GIST Education Innovation Award, the Haedong Scholarly Award, the GIST Research Award, and national recognition for top R&D achievements in Korea. He currently serves as an Associate Editor of IEEE Transactions on Cybernetics.

Professional Interest

Professor Lee's professional interests center on teaching, research, and technology commercialization. His teaching interests include machine learning, communications, information theory, and signal processing. His research and start-up interests include machine learning, computational intelligence, intelligent sensors, networks, blockchain technologies, AI agents, and Web3 systems, with emphasis on interdisciplinary applications and real-world deployment.
