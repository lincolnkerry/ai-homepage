The Fall 2026 offering of **Information Theory (EC5301)** opened its
[course homepage](https://lincolnkerry.github.io/infotheory-2026/) this week — and it
is the lab's first fully AI-native course.

Two features stand out. An embedded **AI teaching assistant** answers student
questions on demand; its Commit-Reply mechanism returns answers rendered by a
frontier model, complete with typeset equations. And homework flows through an
**AI grading pipeline**: students push submissions to GitHub, an automated grader
reads the PDF directly — handwritten scans included — and returns per-problem
feedback as a commit to each student's private repository.

The design keeps rigorous material rigorous: quick definitions go to the chatbot,
while proof-level questions run through public GitHub Q&A, so every answered
question becomes a searchable archive for the next student.

Previous years' lectures remain available:
[Information Theory 2022](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLWq4e5y2b_S_uJpwB_2ue0LIwqWyjPXYA) (23 videos)
and [2020](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLWq4e5y2b_S8v4twgygwvmKtdmCzNZU80) (70 videos)
on the lab YouTube channel.
